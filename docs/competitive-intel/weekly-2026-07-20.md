## 1. Academic / arXiv

### Notable this week

**"Towards end-to-end optimization in multimaterial 3D printing"** (arXiv 2607.13174, July 2026)
- One-line: ML-driven end-to-end optimization for multimaterial FDM/SLA, covering process parameter and geometry co-optimization.
- Competitive relevance: Low for orientIQ directly — focuses on material assignment, not build direction. Signals growing appetite for differentiable, end-to-end AM pipelines that could eventually absorb orientation as a sub-problem.
- Source: https://arxiv.org/html/2607.13174

### Recent (within ~30 days, worth tracking)

**Wotten & Kim — "Multi-objective build orientation and topology optimization for cost and time minimization in fiber-reinforced additive manufacturing"** (Struct. Multidisc. Optim. 69, 2026)
- One-line: Simultaneously minimizes compliance and support structures for fiber-reinforced AM by coupling topology optimization with build orientation optimization; uses multi-objective Pareto framework.
- Competitive relevance: **Medium-high.** Directly overlaps with orientIQ's Pareto front and support-minimization approach. Key difference: they optimize topology and orientation jointly (design-phase tool), while orientIQ takes a fixed geometry as input. Not a slicer competitor but shows the research frontier is converging on the same objectives (overhang, build height, cost). Worth reading the gradient formulation.
- Source: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00158-026-04255-4

**"Novel insights into Pareto fronts in multiobjective topology optimization and a comparative study of scalarization strategies"** (arXiv 2606.16792, June 2026)
- One-line: Systematic comparison of scalarization methods (weighted sum, ε-constraint, Chebyshev) on topology + orientation Pareto problems.
- Competitive relevance: Low directly, but the benchmarking of scalarization strategies is relevant to how orientIQ's λ sweep is positioned vs. alternatives.
- Source: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.16792

**No papers found** this week specifically mentioning "Hopf fibration + 3D printing" or "Gauss map + additive manufacturing." The S²-reduction angle remains orientIQ's distinctive framing.

## 2. Slicer Releases

**Netfabb 2026** (Autodesk, released spring 2026; Autodesk Community post active this week)
- One-line: New "True Shape" packing engine (serial production focus), redesigned interface surfacing support-generation and orientation tools, expanded support-orientation control to prevent deformation, upgraded JS engine for automation scripting.
- Competitive relevance: **Medium.** The orientation UI surface improvement and True Shape packing encroach on orientIQ's build-plate-setup UX story. Netfabb is enterprise-only and metal-AM focused — not a direct slicer competitor for FDM users. Watch True Shape packing if orientIQ ever adds multi-part bin-packing.
- Source: https://3dprintingindustry.com/news/autodesk-updates-netfabb-with-enhanced-workflow-tools-and-new-3mf-support-in-2026-release-238996/

## 3. Startup / Industry News

**Castor Technologies — confirmed closed** (liquidation filed Tel Aviv District Court, June 2025; confirmed shut by founder)
- One-line: Israeli AM software startup that did CAD-file printability analysis and orientation guidance filed for court-ordered liquidation owing ~$2.3M; founder has confirmed the company has ended.
- Competitive relevance: **Notable.** Removes a geometry-aware orientation competitor from the market. Their closure (blamed on slowdown in industrial AM software investment starting 2023) is a useful data point on market conditions. Their former users are looking for alternatives.
- Source: https://3dprint.com/319042/israeli-3d-printing-software-startup-castor-files-for-liquidation/

**Atlas 3D / Sunata** — absorbed into Siemens (acquired 2019), no new news this week. Sunata is now part of Siemens NX AM workflows targeting metal SLM — not a direct competitor for orientIQ's Python-library / FDM positioning.
