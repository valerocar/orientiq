## 1. Academic Papers

### Notable — Springer 2026 (recent, exact publication date unconfirmed this week)

**"Multi-objective build orientation and topology optimization for cost and time
minimization in fiber-reinforced additive manufacturing"**
*Structural and Multidisciplinary Optimization* — doi:10.1007/s00158-026-04255-4

- **What:** Simultaneously co-optimizes topology and build orientation in fiber-reinforced
  AM (FRAM), minimizing compliance and support volume. Uses Pareto-style multi-objective
  framework (likely NSGA-II family).
- **Why it matters:** Directly overlaps with orientIQ's objective set (support volume,
  build height, surface quality) and uses Pareto analysis. Key difference: the paper
  couples orientation to topology and anisotropic fiber behavior — a harder, different
  problem. orientIQ works on fixed geometry with analytical S² gradients; this is
  topology-design-phase work, not slicer-prep-phase. No direct competitive threat but
  signals growing academic legitimacy of multi-objective orientation as a field.
- [Link](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00158-026-04255-4)

---

**"Comprehensive additive manufacturing cost reduction through simultaneous topology
and build orientation optimization"**
*Progress in Additive Manufacturing* — doi:10.1007/s40964-025-01520-8

- **What:** Uses four physical cost proxies — build height, surface area, overhang area,
  support structure volume — to jointly optimize topology and orientation. Published in
  2025/2026.
- **Why it matters:** This is the closest academic work to orientIQ's exact objective
  set. The objectives map almost one-to-one to orientIQ's `objectives.py`. The approach
  is still topology-coupled (not pure orientation search on fixed mesh), so it targets
  the design phase, not the prep-for-slicing workflow. But any downstream simplification
  to fixed-mesh orientation search would produce a direct academic competitor.
  Worth monitoring the authors for follow-up work.
- [Link](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40964-025-01520-8)

## 4. Patents

### US 12,403,662 — "Determining build orientation to minimize thermal distortion"

- **What:** Uses moment-of-inertia as a cheap proxy for thermal distortion to score
  candidate orientations, avoiding full FEA per candidate. Originally filed as
  WO2021127201A1 (PCT application 2020). US grant appears recent (2025–2026).
- **Why it matters:** Orthogonal to orientIQ — this targets metal LPBF thermal
  warping, not FDM/SLA overhang and surface quality. The moment-of-inertia proxy is
  a rough heuristic; orientIQ's analytical S² gradients are more mathematically
  rigorous for geometry-based objectives. No direct overlap with orientIQ's target
  workflow.
- [USPTO PDF](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/12403662)

### US 12,343,928 — "Build orientation for additive manufacturing of complex structures"

- **What:** Orientation method targeting parts with internal channels/orthogonal
  surfaces where internal supports are hard to remove. Priority from 2018 provisional.
- **Why it matters:** Narrow claim scope (specific part geometry class). Not a
  general-purpose orientation optimizer. No competitive concern.
- [USPTO PDF](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/12343928)

## Summary

A quiet week for direct competitors. The two 2026 Springer papers are the most
relevant signal — the academic community is converging on the same multi-objective
objective set (overhang, build height, support volume) orientIQ uses, but all current
work couples orientation to topology optimization at the design phase. orientIQ's niche
— fast, analytically rigorous orientation search on a fixed mesh for slicing prep —
remains uncontested in both commercial slicers and the open literature.

**Watch list for next week:** Check whether either Springer paper has an open-access
preprint on arXiv; monitor OrcaSlicer and PrusaSlicer GitHub for any PR merging
gradient-based auto-orient.
