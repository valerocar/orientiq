## 1. arXiv / Academic Papers

### WildFab: Multi-Axis 3D Printing from Models in the Wild
- **What it is:** arXiv:2609.02413 (submitted Sept 2, 2026). Takes noisy, non-watertight meshes and generates multi-axis robotic toolpaths using a hybrid neural unsigned-distance-field + generalized winding-number representation.
- **Competitive relevance:** Targets the harder problem of *multi-axis* printing (5-axis robotic), not standard FDM orientation. orientIQ's S²-reduction insight only applies to single-axis (gravity-aligned) printers. No direct conflict, but confirms growing academic interest in geometry-driven orientation on non-planar printers — a space orientIQ does not yet occupy.
- **Source:** [arXiv:2609.02413](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.02413)

### Multi-Objective Build Orientation + Topology Optimization for Fiber-Reinforced AM
- **What it is:** Journal paper in *Structural and Multidisciplinary Optimization* (DOI 10.1007/s00158-026-04255-4, 2026). Simultaneously optimizes compliance and support structure for fiber-reinforced parts.
- **Competitive relevance:** Closest academic parallel to orientIQ's multi-objective Pareto approach. Key difference: it co-optimizes topology *and* orientation (design-time decision), whereas orientIQ optimizes orientation for a fixed geometry (print-time decision). Different use case, not a direct substitute — but a research group working on this may extend it toward orientIQ's niche.
- **Source:** [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00158-026-04255-4)

### Nothing on Hopf Fibration or Gauss Map for 3D Printing
- No papers mentioning these terms in the context of print orientation appeared in the last 7 days or in recent 2026 searches. orientIQ's use of the Hopf fibration to reduce SO(3) → S² remains academically novel.
